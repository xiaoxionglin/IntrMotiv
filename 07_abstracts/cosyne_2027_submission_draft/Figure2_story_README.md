## A: behavior examples and swappable statistics

The trajectory thumbnails show 10,001-observation frozen own-policy probes at 100 million training frames. They keep the source segment colors and a common spatial extent. NG is seed 99, UG is seed 123, and FG is seed 17. The UG example was changed after comparing all five terminal own-policy probes: seed 123 has far less repeated circling than seed 99 and visits 312 versus 190 of 361 bins. Its probe path efficiency is 0.062 versus 0.012 for seed 99; seed 41 is slightly higher at 0.079 but visits only 205 bins. These examples are illustrative, not matched-seed or median examples. The [UG seed-123 full plot](figure2_candidates/UG_s123_100m_trajectory.png) was rendered with the canonical spatial plotter from its stored pose trace; its source path and hash are in the manifest.

The path-efficiency chart uses **five terminal training seeds per design** (8, 17, 41, 99, 123) from the canonical [trajectory summary](../../06_experiments/results/cosyne_2027_checkpoint_fields/graph_trajectory_summary/trajectory_by_snapshot.csv). Each design uses temporal DG credit; UG also uses temporal worker-hit credit. Path efficiency is the sum of each independent segment's start-to-end displacement divided by total traveled distance across all segments. The colored dots are seeds and the black bars are arithmetic means, following the Bernstein poster's compact dot-plot style. They are descriptive summaries, not confidence intervals. **The dots come from the latest 100,000 online own-policy observations near 100 million training frames; the adjacent trajectory examples are separate 10,001-observation frozen-policy probes at those checkpoints.**

## B–E: retained comparisons and examples

**B, behavior ablation:** The cross denotes replacement of learned actions with uniform random actions while the network remains frozen. All five B plots show learned-policy minus random-action differences. DG spatial information is in bits per **decision**; readout MLP layer-1 spatial information is in bits per **activation**, and readout-layer split-half spatial-map reliability is a correlation. The information comparison uses equally weighted shared spatial bins and jointly eligible units; readout reliability uses each policy's native occupied bins. The new DG map-cosine statistic is the mean cosine over distinct pairs of jointly field-eligible DG units' occupancy-corrected rate maps, on bins visited at least ten times under both policies in each evaluation repeat. It does not pool all DG pairs regardless of activity. The new field-peak coverage statistic divides the exponential entropy of equal-mass, Gaussian-smoothed dominant peak locations by the number of common visited bins. It requires the same jointly eligible units to be active in at least 0.25% of decisions in both arms. The Gaussian width is 100 DMLab units. Each colored dot is one complete terminal model: 10 NG, 20 UG, and eight FG. Two paired 50,000-decision evaluation repeats were averaged **within model** before plotting. Two additional FG models lacked complete probes and are absent from every B box. The common-bin mask and matching-unit requirement make the geometry comparison fairer, but the policies still visit different trajectories and may sample each common bin differently. A smaller cosine means less map overlap; a smaller coverage fraction means more concentrated dominant peaks. Neither sign is inherently a performance improvement.

**C, push ablation:** Temporal minus mean-matched constant DG credit across five matched seeds per design. The first three representation measures compare trained networks on the same 10,001 observations within each pair; reliable graph edges come from separate own-policy snapshots. The worker setting is held fixed within each pair, and UG keeps temporal worker-hit credit.

**D, pull ablation:** Temporal minus constant worker-hit credit while DG credit remains temporal, across five matched UG and FG seeds at PPO discount $\gamma=0.999$ and GAE $\lambda=0.995$. The representation and graph measures come from separate policy-driven 100,000-decision snapshots and therefore include trajectory differences. The push and pull cohorts have different policy-credit settings, so equal-looking vertical positions do not imply equal effect magnitudes.

**E, DG fields:** Units 4, 7, 12, and 15 come from a separate C15 temporal DG-credit, seed-123 frozen-policy probe near 100 million frames. Its 10,001 decisions visited 307 of 361 bins. Gray denotes unvisited bins; all four maps use the source atlas's common within-run color scale. These examples do not enter A–D. Unit 4 meets the archived strict single-component criterion; the other three show different spatial responses.

## Concise caption draft

**Policy behavior and temporal credit shape spatial representations.** **A,** Example NG, UG, and FG terminal 10k-probe trajectories (seeds 99, 123, and 17) and path efficiency from separate 100k online windows across five training seeds per design. Path efficiency is summed segment displacement divided by total path length; colored dots are seeds and black bars are means. **B,** With networks frozen, learned-policy minus uniform-random-action differences are shown for DG spatial information (bits/decision), readout MLP layer-1 spatial information (bits/activation), DG map cosine, field-peak coverage (fraction of common occupied bins), and readout layer-1 split-half spatial-map reliability. Each of 10 NG, 20 UG, and eight FG dots is one terminal model averaged over two paired 50k-decision probes. Map cosine averages distinct pairs of jointly field-eligible DG rate maps on bins visited at least ten times in both arms; peak coverage summarizes equal-mass, Gaussian-smoothed dominant peaks of matched active units. Thus cosine and coverage use matched spatial support but the policies' observations and trajectories differ. **C,** Temporal versus mean-matched constant DG credit (push), with five matched seeds per design and the same observations within each pair for representation measures. **D,** Temporal versus constant worker-hit credit (pull), with temporal DG credit fixed and five matched UG and FG seeds at jointly raised PPO discount and GAE trace settings; these representation and graph measures use each policy's own trajectories. **E,** Four example DG maps (units 4, 7, 12, and 15) from a separate FG temporal-credit model (seed 123); gray bins were unvisited. In B–D, vertical axes give the labeled difference $\Delta$; dots represent models or matched seed pairs, boxes show quartiles and medians, and whiskers show observed extrema. A positive difference is a larger measured value under learned actions in B or temporal credit in C–D; it does not by itself establish better control. Scales differ by metric, and boxes are descriptive rather than significance intervals.
