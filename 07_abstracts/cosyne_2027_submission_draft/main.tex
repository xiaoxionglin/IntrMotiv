\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries Spatial representations emerge from temporal event credit in a hippocampus-inspired navigation agent without spatial supervision}\par
\end{center}
\vspace{-5pt}

Can spatial representations emerge from sensory inputs and learning objectives that do not explicitly encode space? We investigate this question using a bottom-up computational architecture inspired by hippocampal physiology, exploring whether spatial organization can arise from the temporal structure of sensory experience and self-generated behavior, without position labels, maps, or spatial rewards.

Our model combines dentate gyrus (DG)-like sparse sensory events with CA3-like sequential memory. Temporal relationships between encountered DG events provide two complementary learning signals: a \textbf{``push''} that reinforces DG activity more strongly after longer intervals between events, and a \textbf{``pull''} that rewards shorter transitions between sensory events or, in goal-conditioned variants, faster arrival at internally defined DG targets. Together, these pressures aim to make temporal separation a more informative proxy for navigational distance. The agent learns from egocentric visual experience and intrinsic motivations, without explicit information about its physical location or spatial distances.

During exploration of a continuous visual environment, spatially informative DG activity emerges. To isolate the contribution of temporal information, we compare temporally weighted DG feedback against mean-matched constant feedback, evaluating the resulting representations on identical sensory observations. Temporal feedback produces higher DG spatial information, less redundant response maps, and a broader spatial distribution of field peaks across five paired training seeds. These improvements extend to downstream decoder representations, where spatial information also increases consistently. In contrast, temporally weighting the policy's target-hit reward does not consistently improve exploration or spatial representation quality compared with constant hit rewards.

The agent's behavior further shapes spatial coding. Even with network weights frozen, the learned policy produces more spatially informative downstream activity than random actions, demonstrating that spatial coding depends not only on the learned network but also on how the agent interacts with its environment.

Together, our results demonstrate how spatial coding can emerge from the interaction of temporal learning, sequential memory, and self-generated behavior, without explicitly optimizing for spatial information.

\textbf{Architecture and learning signals.} Egocentric RGB images pass through an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 trunk fixed through layer 2. A trainable projection, batch normalization, and thresholded rectification then produce sparse dentate gyrus (DG) events. A fixed CA3-like shift memory retains their recent sequence. Its flattened state, together with egocentric depth, feeds a learned two-layer, 128-unit multilayer perceptron (MLP) readout that estimates value and selects among five actions. Temporal DG credit reinforces eligible event responses according to the elapsed number of decisions since a preceding event (push). Policy reward favors shorter event transitions or, in goal-conditioned variants, internally detected DG target hits (pull). C01 explores directly, C05 uses a DG target, and C15 adds frontier-based target choice. The agent receives no position coordinates, map, or spatial reward during learning (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}).

\textbf{Three tests.} We measure spatial coding along evolving trajectories, change actions with network weights fixed, and replay identical observations through separately trained networks (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}). These comparisons distinguish the emergence of a spatial code during learning, its expression under different behavior, and the contribution of temporal DG credit to the learned network. Common replay removes evaluation-sampling differences, although training paths may still matter.

\textbf{Spatial emergence and temporal push.} DG coding rises online as the agent explores (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}A). An archived C01 rollout and two individual DG response maps illustrate what a trajectory and spatial fields look like in this environment (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}C); this separate run is not part of the matched credit comparison. With the same 10,001 observations replayed through each trained network, temporal DG credit improves the baseline DG spatial score in all five matched seeds (mean $+0.0575$), reduces map overlap, and broadens the area covered by eligible field peaks (mean $+0.109$; Fig.~\ref{fig:results}D). Readout MLP layer 1 gains spatial information in all five baseline pairs (mean $+0.680$ bits per activation; Fig.~\ref{fig:results}E). Its effect also has positive means for both C05 worker arms and C15, although one of the ten C05 pairs and one of the five C15 pairs reverse direction.

\textbf{Behavior shapes spatial coding.} With each network frozen, its learned policy elicits more spatially informative readout MLP layer 1 activity and more reliable spatial maps than random actions in all 38 matched models (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}B). Thus, behavior changes the spatial signal available from a fixed network. This test identifies an effect of the agent's interaction with the environment at evaluation time; it does not by itself show how policy changes shaped the network during training.

\textbf{Temporal pull.} Adding temporal weighting to the C05 policy's target-hit reward has mixed effects on visited coverage and online DG spatial information when encoder credit is held fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}F). Both worker bonuses reward detector hits, and actor--critic discounting already favors earlier hits. A separate longer-credit intervention jointly raises PPO discount from $\gamma=0.99$ to $0.999$ and GAE $\lambda$ from $0.95$ to $0.99$. Coverage AUC rises in all three prescribed-goal and all three C15 seed pairs, whereas frozen-rollout learned-unit spatial information falls in all six. Those frozen evaluations use separate policy-driven paths, so they do not isolate a fixed-input change in DG coding. Detector hits and coverage do not establish physical goal arrival; matched-command and physical-arrival analyses are needed to assess goal control.
