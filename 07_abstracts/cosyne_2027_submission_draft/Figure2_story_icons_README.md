B places the selected 0.300-amplitude Perlin spatial-information icon after that plot and the 10 × 20 mm split-half DG reliability icon after its reliability box plot. C and D place the same spatial-information icon, then overlap, Gaussian peak-coverage, and reliable-directed-edge icons after their corresponding plots. Their TS boxes sit lower, with both directed arrows meeting the bottom edges of DG and policy. The overlap icon is 10.5 × 20 mm; coverage and edge instances are approximately 6.7 × 20.1 mm. Upward metric arrows share one final-size head, stroke, and length; visible B–D y labels have a uniform gap to their axes. All statistical plots retain their dimensions.

In every icon, the **upper example has the higher raw statistic**, and the arrow points up like the plot y-axis. In A's icon, open and filled circles mark the same start and end in both routes; the dashed chord below shows displacement while the dark detour shows traveled length. Colored circles identify units in the coverage and graph icons. Overlap places two distinct colored maps labeled DG 1 and DG 2 side by side, like the reliability icon's T1/T2 map pair; neither paired-map icon uses separate identity dots. C and D report paired differences, not the depicted examples: temporal minus constant DG credit in C, and temporal minus constant policy reward in D. In D, NG varies flat-policy reward while UG and FG vary target-hit reward. B reports learned policy minus random actions. NG has five paired seed dots and a Tukey box in D’s DG-information, map-overlap, and peak-coverage plots. NG is explicitly N/A in the C/D reliable-edge panels, with no box, dot, or numeric value. ## Caption draft

**Policy behavior and temporal credit shape spatial representations.** **A,** NG, UG, and FG trajectory examples show 65 complete fragments sampled from separate terminal 100k-observation own-policy windows. The color scale gives the step within each fragment, restarting at zero for the next fragment; the examples do not show the full windows. Path efficiency uses the full windows from five runs per design. The schematic compares segment displacement (dashed chord) with traveled length (solid route); the plotted score sums displacement across segments and divides by total length. **B,** Frozen-network learned-policy minus random-action differences in DG spatial information and split-half map reliability across 38 terminal models; two paired 50k-decision evaluations are averaged within each model. **C,** Temporal minus mean-matched constant DG credit (push), five matched seeds per design. DG representation readouts use identical observations within each pair, while stored graph edges use separate own-policy snapshots. **D,** Temporal minus constant policy reward (pull), with temporal DG credit fixed, in five matched seeds per design at PPO discount $\gamma=0.999$ and GAE $\lambda=0.995$. NG changes flat-policy reward on eligible decisions, using a constant calibrated at 0–2M frames; UG and FG change target-hit bonuses. These are within-design contrasts with different reward recipients, and the NG terminal reward levels are not mean-matched. Representation and graph readouts use separate own-policy snapshots. The eligible-peak coverage measure alone uses a pooled 318-bin spatial mask across all 30 arm snapshots. NG has no applicable reliable-edge comparison in C or D, so those positions show N/A without plotted values. In B–D, dots are models or matched seed pairs, boxes show quartiles and medians, whiskers reach the most extreme observations within $1.5\times\mathrm{IQR}$ of the box, and the horizontal line marks zero. All observations, including those beyond the whiskers, remain visible as dots. Icons right of the spatial-information, reliability, map-overlap, peak-coverage, and reliable-edge plots illustrate each measure; their upward arrows indicate a higher raw value, not an observed effect. The spatial-information icon shows one localized DG field above a diffuse Perlin-textured field with an added lower-map tint; the schematic does not match their total activity. Reliability compares the same DG unit at T1 and T2; overlap compares distinct units labeled DG 1 and DG 2. The four peak-coverage blurs use their corresponding unit colors. Map overlap is mean pairwise cosine between active-unit spatial maps. Reliable edges describe stored directed graph structure, not executed goal arrival.
