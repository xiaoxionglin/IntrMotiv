The newest standalone [Figure 2 PDF](Figure2.pdf) and [editable SVG](Figure2.svg) use the selected H colors: orange push and violet pull. In H, illustrative DG identities 3 and 4 are green and cyan; the caption below describes the original-color base SVG.

This **7.5 × 3.19-inch** option removes panel E from the [story layout](Figure2_story_README.md) and uses its width for schematic metric icons. Every icon sits immediately to the **right of its corresponding statistic**. A places its 10 × 20 mm path-efficiency icon after the dot plot, aligned with the lower-row left margin; its three 39-point trajectory tiles still show 65 complete sampled fragments from separate 100k-observation own-policy windows. A shared scale labels the local step within each fragment. The figure labels NG, UG, and FG without run identifiers, and the path-efficiency axis label uses regular weight. B places the 10 × 20 mm split-half DG reliability icon after its reliability box plot. C and D each place overlap, Gaussian peak-coverage, and reliable-directed-edge icons after their corresponding plots. Their TS boxes sit lower, with both directed arrows meeting the bottom edges of DG and policy. The overlap icon is 10.5 × 20 mm; coverage and edge instances are approximately 6.7 × 20.1 mm. All statistical plots retain their dimensions.

In every icon, the **upper example has the higher raw statistic**, and the arrow points up like the plot y-axis. In A's icon, open and filled circles mark the same start and end in both routes; the dashed chord below shows displacement while the dark detour shows traveled length. Colored circles identify units in the coverage and graph icons. Overlap places two distinct colored maps labeled DG 1 and DG 2 side by side, like the reliability icon's T1/T2 map pair; neither paired-map icon uses separate identity dots. Reliability compares the same red unit at two times. Each coverage peak has a wider blur in its unit color. The icons are schematics and contain no measured values or result directions. C and D report paired differences, not the depicted examples: temporal minus constant DG credit in C, and temporal minus constant worker-hit credit in D. B reports learned policy minus random actions. [Individual SVG/PDF/PNG icons](Figure2_metric_icons_README.md) remain available for manual placement or replacement in Overleaf.

## Caption draft

**Policy behavior and temporal credit shape spatial representations.** **A,** NG, UG, and FG trajectory examples show 65 complete fragments sampled from separate terminal 100k-observation own-policy windows. The color scale gives the step within each fragment, restarting at zero for the next fragment; the examples do not show the full windows. Path efficiency uses the full windows from five runs per design. The schematic compares segment displacement (dashed chord) with traveled length (solid route); the plotted score sums displacement across segments and divides by total length. **B,** Frozen-network learned-policy minus random-action differences in DG spatial information and split-half map reliability across 38 terminal models; two paired 50k-decision evaluations are averaged within each model. **C,** Temporal minus mean-matched constant DG credit (push), five matched seeds per design. DG representation readouts use identical observations within each pair, while stored graph edges use separate own-policy snapshots. **D,** Temporal minus constant worker-hit credit (pull), with temporal DG credit fixed, in five matched UG and FG seeds at PPO discount $\gamma=0.999$ and GAE $\lambda=0.995$; representation and graph readouts use separate own-policy snapshots. In B–D, dots are models or matched seed pairs, boxes show quartiles and medians, whiskers show observed extrema, and the horizontal line marks zero. Icons left of the reliability, map-overlap, peak-coverage, and reliable-edge plots illustrate each measure; their upward arrows indicate a higher raw value, not an observed effect. Reliability compares the same DG unit at T1 and T2; overlap compares distinct units labeled DG 1 and DG 2. The four peak-coverage blurs use their corresponding unit colors. Map overlap is mean pairwise cosine between active-unit spatial maps. Reliable edges describe stored directed graph structure, not executed goal arrival.
