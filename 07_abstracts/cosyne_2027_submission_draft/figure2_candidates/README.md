## Shared visual conventions and intervention labels

In candidates 1 and 3, open gray squares are controls, colored circles are tested conditions, and each thin line connects the same training seed or two evaluations of one frozen model. Putting categories on the horizontal axis and measured values on the vertical axis changes only the visual orientation. The zero baseline is shown for nonnegative linear metrics. Detector-hit rates in candidate 3 span roughly two orders of magnitude between prescribed-goal and C15 agents, so that one axis is explicitly logarithmic and includes every observation.

In candidate 2, every colored dot is a paired-seed difference and the black vertical mark is its mean. The gray vertical line is zero. **The horizontal ranges differ by metric**, so dot distance is comparable within a panel, not across panels. C01, C05-wT, C05-wC, and C15 in the left column compare temporal versus mean-matched constant **DG encoder credit** with the worker setting fixed; `wT/wC` identifies that fixed worker hit bonus. C05-eT/eC in the right column compare temporal versus mean-matched constant **worker hit credit** with DG encoder credit fixed; `eT/eC` identifies that fixed encoder setting. The C05 pairs reuse the same five training seeds across factorial arms; those arms are not additional independent replicates.

Information per activation is the occupancy-weighted field score divided by a unit's mean positive response and averaged over field-eligible units (at least 20 positive observations in at least three spatial bins). The amplitude-weighted bits-per-step score averages **all** channels, including silent channels as zero. Changes in response gain, occupancy, eligibility, or silence can separate these measures. CA3 includes the DG slots in its 1,136-channel state, while each readout MLP layer has 128 channels. All DG/CA3/readout comparisons in candidate 2 replay one saved 10,001-observation input and action-history sequence through separate 100M-frame terminal networks. The shared input removes evaluation-trajectory differences; trained weights and internal state dynamics still differ.

## Candidate 1 caption: current A–F evidence with vertical paired plots

**A,** Online amplitude-weighted DG spatial score over active units, plotted against millions of training environment frames for C01, C05, and C15 agents with temporal DG credit; C05 and C15 also have temporal worker hit credit. Curves are five-training-seed means with a translucent ±1 standard error band on each policy's latest 10,000 observations. Network and behavior both change over training. **B,** Frozen readout MLP layer 1 spatial information in bits per activation and split-half spatial-map correlation under random actions versus the model's learned policy. The 38 model pairs comprise 10 C01, 20 C05, and eight C15 terminal models; each value averages two 50,000-decision evaluations. Equal-weight shared spatial bins and jointly eligible units are used for information. Correlation is computed on bins visited at least ten times in each episode-disjoint half of both policies. The intervention changes executed actions with weights fixed.

**C,** DG peak locations from C01 seed 8 under temporal and constant DG credit on the same 10,001 observations. This seed has the largest effective peak-area difference among five matched baseline pairs, so it illustrates an upper-end case rather than a typical one. Orange dots mark active-unit peaks after the 0.25% activity cutoff; grayscale is Gaussian-smoothed peak density on shared visited bins. The displayed effective-area fractions are 0.384 and 0.230. **D,** Original control and tested values for DG amplitude-weighted spatial score, active-map cosine overlap, and effective peak area: C01, C05-wT, C05-wC, and C15 each have five matched seed pairs. A lower active-map cosine means less overlap. Peak area is the exponential entropy of equal-mass peak density divided by 242 visited bins, using a Gaussian width of 100 world-coordinate units.

**E,** Readout MLP layer 1 bits per activation under temporal versus constant DG credit on the same saved replay, with the worker arm fixed. The panel includes all five matched seeds for each of four architecture/factor groups. **F,** C05 temporal versus mean-matched constant worker hit bonus, with either temporal (`eT`) or constant (`eC`) encoder credit fixed. Online visited-cell fraction and amplitude-weighted DG spatial score are averaged over 90–99 million training frames. The bottom row is a **separate** episode-long orthogonal-FiLM study: jointly raising PPO discount from $\gamma=0.99$ to $0.999$ and GAE $\lambda$ from $0.95$ to $0.99$ increases online coverage AUC but decreases learned-unit DG information on separate 10,000-decision, policy-driven frozen rollouts in all three prescribed-goal and all three C15 seed pairs. Different frozen paths prevent a fixed-input representation inference.

The vertical arrangement gives the measured quantity a conventional y-axis and lets the marker directions read upward or downward. Its cost is height: the selected spatial-map example and six separate dot-plot metrics occupy more than a submission-page figure.

## Candidate 2 caption: matched representation matrix

Rows 1–2 compare DG amplitude-weighted spatial score and active-unit map cosine. Rows 3–5 compare field-eligible information per activation in CA3, readout MLP layer 1, and readout MLP layer 2. Rows 6–8 compare the same three layers using the all-unit, amplitude-weighted bits-per-step measure. The left column changes DG encoder credit with worker bonus fixed and has five matched seed pairs in each of C01, C05-wT, C05-wC, and C15. The right column changes worker hit credit with encoder credit fixed and has five C05-eT and five C05-eC pairs. DG replay is measured on the identical observation sequence at terminal checkpoints; the layers use the same saved panel. Positive and negative signed differences are both shown, with zero explicitly marked. A positive map-cosine difference means **more** overlap and is not an improvement; other activity and information measures also have different interpretations. No statistical significance is implied by the black mean mark.

This is the clearest candidate for checking whether an apparently clean readout MLP layer 1 result extends across layers and metric definitions. It shows the five-of-five negative worker effect on weighted readout MLP layer 1 and layer 2 scores under temporal encoder credit, and the mixed CA3 normalized result. The price is a tall matrix and signed-effect axes rather than direct control and tested values.

## Candidate 3 caption: behavior and control outcomes

**A,** Same frozen own-policy-versus-random intervention as candidate 1B: readout MLP layer 1 information and map reliability from 38 terminal models, each averaged over two evaluations. **B,** C05 temporal versus mean-matched constant worker hit bonus, with encoder credit fixed, on three distinct measurements: online visited-cell fraction and DG spatial score over 90–99 million training frames; DG score on one identical 10,001-observation replay at 100 million frames. Each row has five paired training seeds. The online path distribution changes with policy; the common replay holds its external observations fixed. **C,** Stored directed-graph reachable-pair fraction and prospective-success fraction at terminal checkpoints, plus path efficiency from policy-generated trajectories. These are five-seed C05 worker contrasts at each fixed encoder setting. Stored-graph prospects and path efficiency describe these particular sampled structures and trajectories; neither demonstrates executed, physical goal arrival. **D,** Separate episode-long orthogonal-FiLM prescribed-goal and C15 runs: baseline versus jointly longer PPO discount and GAE credit at 100 million frames, with three paired seeds per family. The panels show online coverage AUC, internally detected hits per 1,000 decisions on a log axis, and learned-unit frozen DG information on separate policy-driven 10,000-decision rollouts. Coverage and detector hits increase, while frozen DG information falls. Prescribed-goal and C15 hit rates have different absolute ranges; the log axis keeps both visible. This does not isolate discounting from GAE or representation from the changed rollout distribution.

This layout makes protocol boundaries explicit. It also exposes the mixed or negative worker findings that a coverage-only figure would hide. Its graph and trajectory panels are diagnostic and require care: the existing matched-command and physical-arrival analyses have not established goal control.

## Candidate 4 caption: broad result-direction inventory

The seven blocks inventory every metric-by-group contrast in the selected archived **online, DG replay, peak-spread, downstream-layer, C05 worker-outcome, frozen-behavior, and longer-PPO-credit tables**. For each cell, the text is the count of positive paired differences divided by the number measured. Blue means more positive than negative pairs, orange more negative than positive, and gray a balance or ties; color does **not** encode effect magnitude or scientific desirability. A blank cell is unmeasured, never zero. The first four contrast columns hold worker setting fixed and compare temporal versus constant DG encoder credit; C05-eT/eC hold encoder setting fixed and compare worker credit; C05-int is the within-seed C05 encoder-by-worker interaction. The behavior block instead compares own-policy versus random actions in frozen models (10 C01, 20 C05, eight C15 models), and the final block compares longer versus baseline joint PPO/GAE credit in three prescribed-goal and three C15 seeds.

All four tested peak-activity cutoffs (0%, 0.025%, 0.25%, 1%) appear, as do positive fractions, silent/eligible counts, and amplitude-weighted scores that would be easy to omit when selecting only spatial-information panels. [metric_inventory.csv](metric_inventory.csv) preserves each row's source metric key, exact signed mean, positive/negative/tied counts, and measured $n$. The atlas cannot answer effect-size questions from color alone: compare magnitudes only within one metric and protocol using that CSV or the underlying paired plots. Online, replay, behavior, trajectory, stored-graph, and longer-credit outcomes must not be pooled into one estimate. Raw telemetry channels, unpublished pending training cells, and evaluator sensitivity sweeps outside the selected source tables are outside this atlas.

## Candidates 5–6: compact vertical values and box plots

Both candidates preserve the **7.5 × 4.48 inch** Figure 2 footprint and the quantitative A/B/D/E/F evidence. Candidate 5 shows original paired values: A is the five-seed online DG course; B shows random-action versus learned-policy readout MLP layer 1 information and split-half reliability for 38 frozen models; C shows the selected C01 seed-8 peak maps under temporal and constant DG credit on common replay; D shows DG score, active-map cosine, and peak area for C01, both fixed-worker C05 settings, and C15; E shows readout MLP layer 1 information for those four architecture/factor groups; F shows C05 worker-credit online coverage/DG and the separate joint-$\gamma$/$\lambda$ longer-credit coverage/frozen-DG results. In B/D/E/F, open squares are control values, filled colored circles are tested values, and thin vertical lines join one model or training-seed pair. The short labels 05T and 05C mean fixed worker temporal/constant in D/E, but fixed encoder temporal/constant in the first two F plots. D and E include all five C15 seed pairs.

Candidate 6 is the submitted layout. It retains A and replaces B/D/E/F with **tested minus control** box plots; each such vertical axis explicitly bears $\Delta$. Each box spans the first to third quartiles, its line is the median, whiskers reach the observed minimum and maximum, and every dot is one paired model or seed difference. The horizontal gray line is zero, and each metric has its own symmetric vertical axis; upward is a larger tested value, which is not always scientifically preferable (for map overlap, lower means less overlap). The paired difference is own-policy minus random actions in B; temporal minus constant DG encoder credit in D/E; temporal minus constant C05 worker-hit credit in the first two F plots; and joint longer PPO/GAE credit minus baseline in the last two F plots. The sample sizes are 10/20/8 frozen models for B, 5/5/5/5 paired seeds for D/E, 5/5 for C05 worker-credit F, and 3/3 for longer-credit F. The boxes summarize few observations in D/E/F, especially longer credit, so the overlaid dots are essential. A positive box is a descriptive direction, not a significance test.

Its C panel uses a **separate archived C01 seed-8 model** at 100 million training frames. The path is the full 10,001-decision policy evaluation in a 2,000-by-2,000-unit arena, and DG 11 and DG 0 are occupancy-corrected response maps from the same evaluation. They were the first and fourth of the four fields ranked by spatial-information score in the archived shared-scale figure ($0.28$ and $0.19$ bits). Both maps retain that figure's common amplitude scale; white cells were unvisited. The tiles were cropped from [the full trajectory SVG](../../../06_experiments/results/A0_poster_analysis_20260926/flat_goal_comparison/c01_seed8/trajectory_full.svg) and [the full place-field SVG](../../../06_experiments/results/A0_poster_analysis_20260926/flat_goal_comparison/c01_seed8/place_fields_shared_scale.svg); only the pale trajectory stroke was darkened for compact display, without changing its coordinates. This older C01 model is not one of the current temporal-versus-constant common-replay networks and does not contribute to D or E. The peak-map intervention example remains available in candidate 5; D retains the full peak-spread contrast.
