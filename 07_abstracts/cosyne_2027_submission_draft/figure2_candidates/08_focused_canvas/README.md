This is a movable asset canvas, not a submitted figure layout. It contains only spatial emergence, the frozen-behavior comparison, an encoder-credit **push** comparison, and the **high-policy-credit temporal-shaping (TS) worker pull** comparison. It was redrawn at that width so its text is not horizontally squashed. The two frozen-behavior components are unchanged copies from the preceding loose canvas. Push and pull each have the same six named readouts: DG spatial information, DG map overlap, field-peak coverage, peak distance, commanded target-hit rate, and reliable graph edges. The earlier broader and scatter canvases remain in `07_loose_canvas/`.

Open the editable SVG **as a document** in Inkscape. Its 15 plot components are separate objects directly under one layer, with no nested SVG viewports or white card backgrounds. The [renderer](render_focused_canvas.py) loads archived seed-level tables and rebuilds every new plot; [the CSV](plotted_seed_differences.csv) contains exactly the paired differences shown.

## Reading the panels

**A, spatial emergence.** Mean online DG spatial-information score in bits per decision across five training seeds for each architecture, with a shaded band of one standard error of the mean. NG, UG, and FG mean no goal (C01), uniform goal (C05), and frontier goal (C15). The horizontal axis is training frames in millions. This curve is an online training diagnostic; it does not share the terminal replay or terminal own-policy snapshot protocol below.

**B, frozen behavior.** The first box is the readout MLP layer 1 spatial information per activation; the second is spatial-map split-half reliability. Each colored dot is one frozen model's learned-policy value minus its random-action value. There are 10 NG, 20 UG, and eight FG models. Each model's two 50,000-decision evaluations are averaged before subtraction. Network weights are fixed, while the behavior policy changes the visited trajectory. The zero line means no change; boxes span the first to third quartiles, the central line is the median, and whiskers reach the observed minimum and maximum.

**C, push: temporal minus mean-matched constant DG encoder credit.** This is the original policy-credit cohort, with PPO discount $\gamma=0.99$ and GAE $\lambda=0.95$. For the first four readouts, NG, UG, and FG each have five matched training-seed pairs (8, 17, 41, 99, 123); UG specifically holds the temporal worker bonus fixed. Terminal encoder networks replay the same 10,001 observations within each pair, so the DG representation contrast has a common input. DG information is the active-unit mean amplitude-weighted information in bits per decision. Map overlap is the mean pairwise cosine of active DG spatial rate maps; a positive difference means *more* overlap. Peak coverage is the effective area fraction from equal-mass dominant unit peaks blurred by a Gaussian of width 100 DMLab units on the shared 242-bin common-replay mask, with the 0.25% active-fraction field cutoff. Peak distance is the median distance from each eligible dominant peak to its nearest other eligible peak, in DMLab units, at the same cutoff. This differs from the older loose canvas's *mean pairwise* peak-distance card; the focused canvas uses the matching nearest-neighbor definition for push and pull.

The push commanded-hit card uses the 90–100M-frame online window: correct target-hit observations divided by eligible target-hit event observations, multiplied by 1,000, then temporal encoder minus constant encoder within a seed. The push reliable-edge card uses the temporal-minus-constant count of reliable stored-graph edges in separate 100M own-policy snapshots. These two outcome readouts have no NG value because NG has no commanded goal or goal graph. UG and FG each have five paired seeds for both. The archived conditional-hit table contained FG seeds 17 and 41; the [additional FG scalar extraction](additional_fg_hit_inputs.json) supplies seeds 8, 99, and 123 from their completed runs. Five of the six newly read arms have 305 qualifying updates, and the seed-123 constant-encoder arm has 302; all its required scalar streams agree on those 302 updates. The FG constant-encoder seeds 8 and 99 use the separately recovered training runs, with a distinct recovery source patch. Hit observations are repeated logged training events, not independent physical goal arrivals. Stored edges describe detector-memory structure, not executed goal success. Unlike the first four push cards, these outcomes use policy-dependent experience.

**D, pull: temporal minus constant worker-hit credit at high policy credit.** Both arms keep temporal DG encoder credit and share $\gamma=0.999$ and GAE $\lambda=0.995$; these policy parameters were raised together relative to C. Each UG and FG box contains five paired training seeds (8, 17, 41, 99, 123). The first four readouts come from each model's separate own-policy, 100,000-decision snapshot at 100M frames. Spatial information is again active-unit mean amplitude-weighted bits per decision; overlap is mean active-only map cosine. Peak coverage follows the same equal-mass Gaussian entropy-area formula and 0.25% cutoff as C, but uses a **318-bin pooled visited mask** across the 20 high-credit snapshots. The denominator therefore differs from C, and the absolute area fractions are not directly comparable across those cohorts. Peak distance uses the same eligible-unit median nearest-neighbor definition as C. The pull hit-rate card uses the online 90–100M-frame window and the same type of numerator and eligible-event denominator as the push hit-rate card, then subtracts constant from temporal worker credit. The pull reliable-edge card uses the 100M own-policy stored graph. These snapshots follow different trajectories between arms, so they do not isolate a fixed-input representation effect.

All C and D vertical axes explicitly show $\Delta=\text{temporal}-\text{constant}$ in the named unit. Dots are matched seed differences with 0.7 opacity; box outlines, whiskers, and medians appear above them. The zero line is a reference. Boxes show quartiles and observed minimum-to-maximum whiskers, **not confidence intervals**, and no significance test is encoded. Each card has its own symmetric vertical scale. Compare a push and pull card's signs or distributions with their different cohorts and protocols in mind; equal plot height does not imply equal effect size. More information or greater peak distance is not automatically better control, and more map overlap indicates greater redundancy rather than an unqualified gain.

## Regeneration and provenance

From the repository root:

```bash
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/cosyne_focused_mpl /home/xiaoxiong/miniforge3/envs/SF_git/bin/python 07_abstracts/cosyne_2027_submission_draft/figure2_candidates/08_focused_canvas/render_focused_canvas.py
```

The manifest records SHA-256 hashes for all input tables, the additional FG extraction, and the sample count for every shown architecture/metric/contrast. The push DG and peak tables are the archived common-panel analysis. The push hit-rate source is `conditional_hit_seed_metrics.csv` plus the additional FG extraction; push edge counts are from `paired_directed_graph.csv` at 100M. The extra FG file records each run directory, event-file size and modification time, the number and range of selected updates, and the two summed counts. Its [read-only extractor](collect_additional_fg_hits.py) uses the same TensorBoard tags and 90–100M window as the archived conditional-hit collector, requiring all six scalar streams and finite values at the selected steps. It can be run on NEMO2 against the existing terminal manifest and writes only a lightweight JSON result. The high-credit pull DG and peak values, hit rates, and edge counts are the source tables documented in the [high-credit worker analysis](../../../../06_experiments/reward_credit/results/cosyne27_worker_regimen_boxplots_20261013/README.md). The runner validates the expected seed counts and rejects duplicate or nonfinite paired differences.
