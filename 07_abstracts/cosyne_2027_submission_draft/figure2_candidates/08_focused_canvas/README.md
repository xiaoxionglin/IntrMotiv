## Reading the panels

**A, spatial emergence.** Mean online DG spatial-information score in bits per decision across five training seeds for each architecture, with a shaded band of one standard error of the mean. NG, UG, and FG mean no goal (C01), uniform goal (C05), and frontier goal (C15). The horizontal axis is training frames in millions. This curve is an online training diagnostic; it does not share the terminal replay or terminal own-policy snapshot protocol below.

**B, frozen behavior.** The four boxes show **DG spatial information in bits per step**, **DG map overlap**, **field-peak coverage**, and **readout MLP layer 1 spatial-map split-half reliability**, in that order. The first three measure DG; the fourth measures a downstream readout layer. Each colored dot is one frozen model's learned-policy value minus its uniform-random-action value. There are 10 NG, 20 UG, and eight FG complete models; two further FG constant-encoder runs lacked complete probes and are excluded from all four boxes. Each model has two paired 50,000-decision evaluation repeats, averaged within model before plotting. The 38 models include multiple factor conditions sharing training-seed numbers; the model and factor condition, rather than the numeric seed alone, identify a dot. Network weights are frozen, while actions and visited trajectories change. The zero line means no change; boxes span the first to third quartiles, the central line is the median, and whiskers reach the observed minimum and maximum.

**B metric definitions and support.** One step is one agent decision. The information comparison uses jointly eligible DG units and equally weighted spatial bins with at least ten visits in *both* policies. Bits per step retains activity amplitude: for unit rate $r_b$ in bin $b$, $J=\sum_b p_b r_b\log_2(r_b/\bar r)$, where $\bar r=\sum_b p_b r_b$. The new cosine and coverage boxes use the same common spatial-bin mask within each paired evaluation repeat; it contains 185–316 of the 361 bins across the 76 pairs. Their DG unit set is the intersection of each policy's field-eligible units (at least 20 active observations in at least three bins), 14–16 units per pair. **Map overlap** is the mean cosine of distinct pairs of these units' occupancy-corrected spatial rate maps, evaluated on the common mask; zero-norm map pairs are omitted. Lower cosine means less overlap among their maps. **Field-peak coverage** additionally requires each selected unit to be active on at least 0.25% of decisions in both policies, leaving 12–16 matched peaks per repeat. For each unit and policy, its dominant peak is the maximum rate on the common mask. Give each peak equal mass, blur by a Gaussian of width 100 DMLab units, normalize the density within the common mask, then divide the exponential of its Shannon entropy by the number of common bins. This is the same *coverage formula* as C and D but uses a pair-specific matched support and matched unit identities; its absolute area fractions are therefore not interchangeable with C's fixed 242-bin mask or D's pooled 318-bin mask. The **readout reliability** comparison uses each policy's native occupied bins, so occupancy differences can affect it.

**B result and limit.** In these complete models, the mean own-minus-random map-cosine difference is −0.068/−0.038/−0.056 for NG/UG/FG; it is negative in 9/10, 16/20, and 8/8 models. Mean field-peak coverage differences are −0.113/−0.063/−0.106, negative in 10/10, 17/20, and 8/8. The information difference is positive on average in each architecture. Thus, on matched spatial support, learned behavior expresses a more informative but more concentrated set of DG peaks than uniform random behavior in this cohort. Common bins and units remove two obvious support changes, but the policies still provide different observations, within-bin sample counts, and temporal sequences; these boxes are behavior-expression comparisons, not fixed-input representation tests. No significance test is encoded.

**C, push: temporal minus mean-matched constant DG encoder credit.** This is the original policy-credit cohort, with PPO discount $\gamma=0.99$ and GAE $\lambda=0.95$. For the first four readouts, NG, UG, and FG each have five matched training-seed pairs (8, 17, 41, 99, 123); UG specifically holds the temporal worker bonus fixed. Terminal encoder networks replay the same 10,001 observations within each pair, so the DG representation contrast has a common input. DG information is the active-unit mean amplitude-weighted information in bits per decision. Map overlap is the mean pairwise cosine of active DG spatial rate maps; a positive difference means *more* overlap. Peak coverage is the effective area fraction from equal-mass dominant unit peaks blurred by a Gaussian of width 100 DMLab units on the shared 242-bin common-replay mask, with the 0.25% active-fraction field cutoff. Peak distance is the median distance from each eligible dominant peak to its nearest other eligible peak, in DMLab units, at the same cutoff. This differs from the older loose canvas's *mean pairwise* peak-distance card; the focused canvas uses the matching nearest-neighbor definition for push and pull.

The push commanded-hit card uses the 90–100M-frame online window: correct target-hit observations divided by eligible target-hit event observations, multiplied by 1,000, then temporal encoder minus constant encoder within a seed. The push reliable-edge card uses the temporal-minus-constant count of reliable stored-graph edges in separate 100M own-policy snapshots. These two outcome readouts have no NG value because NG has no commanded goal or goal graph. UG and FG each have five paired seeds for both. The archived conditional-hit table contained FG seeds 17 and 41; the [additional FG scalar extraction](additional_fg_hit_inputs.json) supplies seeds 8, 99, and 123 from their completed runs. Five of the six newly read arms have 305 qualifying updates, and the seed-123 constant-encoder arm has 302; all its required scalar streams agree on those 302 updates. The FG constant-encoder seeds 8 and 99 use the separately recovered training runs, with a distinct recovery source patch. Hit observations are repeated logged training events, not independent physical goal arrivals. Stored edges describe detector-memory structure, not executed goal success. Unlike the first four push cards, these outcomes use policy-dependent experience.

**D, pull: temporal minus constant worker-hit credit at high policy credit.** Both arms keep temporal DG encoder credit and share $\gamma=0.999$ and GAE $\lambda=0.995$; these policy parameters were raised together relative to C. Each UG and FG box contains five paired training seeds (8, 17, 41, 99, 123). The first four readouts come from each model's separate own-policy, 100,000-decision snapshot at 100M frames. Spatial information is again active-unit mean amplitude-weighted bits per decision; overlap is mean active-only map cosine. Peak coverage follows the same equal-mass Gaussian entropy-area formula and 0.25% cutoff as C, but uses a **318-bin pooled visited mask** across the 20 high-credit snapshots. The denominator therefore differs from C, and the absolute area fractions are not directly comparable across those cohorts. Peak distance uses the same eligible-unit median nearest-neighbor definition as C. The pull hit-rate card uses the online 90–100M-frame window and the same type of numerator and eligible-event denominator as the push hit-rate card, then subtracts constant from temporal worker credit. The pull reliable-edge card uses the 100M own-policy stored graph. These snapshots follow different trajectories between arms, so they do not isolate a fixed-input representation effect.

All vertical axes show the measured difference $\Delta$ in the named unit: B uses learned policy minus uniform random actions; C and D use temporal minus constant credit. Dots use 0.7 opacity; box outlines, whiskers, and medians appear above them. The zero line is a reference. Boxes show quartiles and observed minimum-to-maximum whiskers, **not confidence intervals**, and no significance test is encoded. Each card has its own symmetric vertical scale. Compare a push and pull card's signs or distributions with their different cohorts and protocols in mind; equal plot height does not imply equal effect size. More information or greater peak distance is not automatically better control, and more map overlap indicates greater redundancy rather than an unqualified gain.
