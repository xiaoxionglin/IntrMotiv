# Should the episode-long DG variants enter a distance-reward ablation?

**Archived decision snapshot.** The [unified episode-long DG comparison](../dg_goal_control/episode_long_dg_comparison_20261009.md) is the current scientific result owner and includes the later 150M orthogonal-FiLM evidence. This page preserves the narrower 100M reward-ablation decision and its source references.

**Analysis status, 9 October 2026:** training is complete for the 12 source-clock and 18 goal-value-stop runs at 100M frames. Their 95–100M online metrics and all five retained spatial ages are collected. All 12 source-clock 75M and all 36 paired 100M frozen 10k-decision field rollouts completed, including six matched longer-credit controls. A bounded prescribed matched-command preflight completed but found only one repeated start and no independent geometric arrival measurements. This decision includes paired frozen field and limited executed-command evidence; a replicated control endpoint remains open. No new training batch or recurring monitor was launched for this decision.

## Decision

**Do not start a broad hit-only versus temporal-bonus ablation in the new episode-long variants yet.** The separately launched corrected-core C05/C15 first-wave comparison tests the earlier architecture: five runs completed, while C15 seed 99 failed before 5M. Its three completed C05 hit-only seeds have lower 90–100M coverage than their historical temporal-bonus matches by 17.74, 7.80 and 10.34 points, but the historical source is not preserved and the executed-command endpoint is pending. This is useful context, not a causal estimate for the episode-long variants. In the newer prescribed source-clock runs, goal hits are so late that the temporal bonus is nearly absent already. In the learned-goal variants, logged hits are common, but neither the source-clock correction nor goal-value stopping produces a consistent command-specific control signal across seeds. The value-stop C15 source-clock arm is worse on both online coverage and hit rate in every paired seed.

The one plausible **small** new-variant follow-up is C05 with **continuing value and the source-command clock**, paired with a hit-only arm at the same seeds, age, horizon and critic setting. Its source-distance bonus remains active on many hits, and its 100M exploration gain over C05 nearest-clock replicates across seeds. This is conditional on the frozen command tests or a focused hypothesis about exploration; C05 is not yet a demonstrated controller. C05 nearest-clock pays an even larger bonus, 1.62–1.67 on average, but that bonus can be refreshed by an intervening DG event, so it is a less clean distance-from-command intervention. The newer prescribed source-clock arm is a weak ablation target because removing its residual bonus would barely change the reward on typical hits.

| Newer arm | Hit-only ablation priority | Reason at 100M |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prescribed, source clock, continuing value | Low | The mean paid hit reward is only 1.008–1.033; the temporal term has almost expired on observed hits. |
| C15, source clock, continuing value | Hold | The bonus is active, but learned maps remain fragmented and online command diagnostics give no positive control signal. |
| C05, source clock, continuing value | Conditional small test | The bonus is active and coverage AUC rises in all three seeds against its matched nearest-clock control; command-specific control remains unproven. |
| Goal-value stopping in any family | Low | There is no replicated control benefit; C15 source-clock coverage and logged hits fall in every paired seed. |

If the C05 source-clock command panel demonstrates reproducible command-specific arrivals, the minimum new training matrix is its three matched hit-only seeds, with the completed source-clock C05 runs as controls. Evaluate both at the same 100M frozen checkpoints and observation windows. Zeroing the bonus changes both timing information and reward magnitude; a magnitude-matched constant-hit bonus would be a second, explicitly separate comparison if the timing mechanism matters. If the matched-command panel stays near zero, the same three-run experiment could only address exploration or hit-frequency effects and should not be sold as a control-learning test.

This triage concerns the **worker hit-distance bonus**. The original DG encoder's temporal interval credit is a separate factor in the [corrected-core reward study](corrected_core_distance_reward_ablation_20261009.md). Neither changing the worker clock nor stopping worker value at a hit isolates that encoder objective. The current evidence does not justify duplicating its held encoder matrix across these newer variants before the first-wave control result is known. Poor learned fields make that encoder question interesting, but field weakness by itself does not identify which reward component caused it.

Selected-goal exposure also differs: C05 has an active target on approximately 99.9% of logged events, whereas prescribed arms range from 35% to 58% and C15 arms from 32% to 74%. The per-run table reports these denominators. Raw hit counts across DG families therefore mix controller availability with goal-following ability; the within-family paired comparisons above are the relevant contrasts. All reported policy and value losses are finite, so the weak command signal is not a numerical training failure.

The prescribed source-clock continuing-value runs recorded only 57, 80 and 75 correct hits across their exported 5M-window learner histories. Their hit-weighted completed-command durations were 331, 304 and 333 decisions, and the paid hit magnitudes were **1.008, 1.033 and 1.008**. The corrected distance differed from the nearest DG age on every logged hit. Thus the clock change is engaged, but removing the temporal term would have a small average reward effect in these prescribed runs. These counts are repeated learner-batch observations and should not be used as confidence intervals for physical arrival. The compact [source-clock hit-weighted table](results/source_distance_hit_dg_20261009/online_95_100m/hit_weighted_95_100m.csv) and [value-stop hit-weighted table](results/goal_value_stop_dg_20261009/online_95_100m/hit_weighted_95_100m.csv) are computed from the exported per-run histories using each update's correct-hit count as its weight; zero-hit updates contribute no conditional mean.

The source-clock C15 continuing-value arms had hit-weighted magnitudes 1.429, 1.348 and 1.405, with mean completed-command durations 41, 48 and 46 decisions. C05 source-clock continuing-value arms paid 1.364, 1.536 and 1.558 after 44, 25 and 20 decisions. C05 nearest-clock controls paid 1.653, 1.624 and 1.671; recent DG activations can keep the nearest-event bonus high even when the command itself is older. This is a real reward-exposure contrast, but it is not evidence that the bonus teaches the correct destination.

The C05 source-clock continuing-value arm has a same-direction **exploration** difference in all three seeds at 100M: coverage AUC is 84.5/62.1/57.3, versus 57.7/42.0/52.5 for the nearest-clock match, gains of +26.7/+20.1/+4.8 by seed. Its logged commanded-hit rate is lower in every seed by 14.2/2.7/3.3 per 1,000 selected-goal events, and retrospective shuffled hits remain close. With only three paired seeds, the range of observed effects is more informative than a narrow-looking interval; even three same-sign pairs have a two-sided exact sign-test probability of 0.25 under a zero-median null. This is a reason to retain C05 source-clock as an exploration candidate, not a demonstration that it follows the commanded goal.

Mechanistically, the worker bonus can only change learning on a hit. Source-clock prescribed hits arrive mostly after its 70-decision support has ended, so a hit-only ablation there would test a nearly inactive term. C05 generates more hit events within the support window and therefore supplies an identifiable reward perturbation. The lower C05 source-clock hit rate alongside higher coverage is also compatible with the nearest-event clock reinforcing short local event chains; that interpretation is a hypothesis because the online counts do not reveal the counterfactual path under another command.

Goal-value stopping did not rescue C15: source-clock coverage AUC changes relative to its continuing-value match were **−13.1, −28.9 and −36.5** points, and hit-rate changes were **−15.2, −10.3 and −14.2** per 1,000 events. Nearest-clock C15 hit rates also fell in all three seeds. Prescribed value-stop hit rates fell in all three seeds under each clock, although coverage changes were mixed. C05 value-stop effects were seed-dependent under both clocks. A lower value loss or a smaller advantage standard deviation in some value-stop arms is not evidence of improved goal following because the return target itself changed.

The stop-value result does not prove that a goal-only critic is conceptually wrong. It says this implementation, with the same sparse hit reward and 100M training budget, did not turn that cleaner target into better observed behavior. Removing post-hit returns may also remove learning credit for reaching states from which later commands are productive. An additional reward ablation inside the value-stop arms would add a second sparse-signal change before any command benefit is established.

## Goal control and what is still missing

At 100M, commanded versus retrospectively shuffled goal labels yield nearly equal online hit rates in the prescribed and C15 arms. The policy's online goal-action probability total variation remains roughly 0.001–0.003 in those arms, and approximately 0.001–0.007 in C05. These observational diagnostics provide no positive command-specific signal; label shuffling cannot establish a causal null.

The previously available 75M exact-start prescribed archive had only one completed short-horizon seed and one episode-long seed, so it cannot give paired three-seed effects. The original source-clock 75M preflight, job 8319859, timed out after one hour without a summary. A [versioned evaluation-only source-shard patch](../../hpc_runs/patches/goal_value_stop_eval_source_sharding_20261009.patch), SHA-256 `2f746470b13d46a1d00649a9ffe3a9e3961b2952270b0e39cad74f0915543c6c`, adapts the earlier bounded-source method to the current evaluator while preserving the StudySpec's target selection, repeats and 900-decision horizon. Six focused tests passed on NEMO2.

The one-source seed-99 prescribed 100M preflight, job 8320616, [completed exit zero](results/source_distance_hit_dg_20261009/frozen_command_preflight_100m/intervention_summary.json) with exact matched starts, frozen policy and graph, and [nine executed rows](results/source_distance_hit_dg_20261009/frozen_command_preflight_100m/intervention_trials.csv): one discovered start, three target fields, and three executed command choices per target. It found only **one of eight requested repeated starts**. Initial action distributions differ by command (mean pairwise total variation 0.083), but all nine trajectories end at the same pose after 520 remaining physical-episode decisions. At this one start, field-detector hits are 0/3 commanded versus 0/6 alternatives by 64 and 128 decisions, 0/3 versus 2/6 by 256, and 1/3 versus 3/6 by the 900-decision bound. The three-target paired lift at the final bound is −0.167; observed hit latencies are 177, 248, 356 and 499 decisions. Five of nine rows are episode-censored after 520 remaining physical-episode decisions. The `physical_start_distance` and `physical_minimum_distance` columns are null in all nine rows, so this is a **detector-event observation**, not an independently verified geometric-arrival estimate. It is too small and source-specific to rank the reward arms by control.

A complete matched-start panel must report commanded versus alternative-command *physical arrivals* at 64, 128, 256 and 900 decisions, latency, failures, episode censoring, source/goal exposure and action changes. The sharded preflight establishes a usable execution path but shows that repeated-start discovery and independent physical-arrival output need repair before scaling that panel. Until then, the replicated control conclusion is unresolved.

## Reusable lesson

For the next reward study, first measure how often the proposed reward term actually changes the return on the goals being evaluated. A source-clock bonus with a very small observed mean contribution is a poor target for a costly hit-only ablation. Use paired seeds and frozen commanded-versus-alternative arrivals to choose a candidate; complete field, segmented-path and graph analyses alongside the online metrics. The exact-start evaluator needs bounded fresh-engine source shards and an audited merge for large panels; a monolithic preflight can time out without any usable trial summary.
