## Title

Spatial representations emerge from temporal experience in a hippocampus-inspired navigation agent without spatial supervision

## Abstract

Can spatial representations emerge from sensory inputs and learning objectives that do not explicitly encode space? We investigate this question using a bottom-up computational architecture inspired by hippocampal physiology, without position labels, maps, or spatial rewards. Rather than imposing spatial structure through an explicit learning objective, we explore whether it can emerge from the temporal organization of sensory experience and the agent's interactions with its environment.

Our model combines sparse dentate gyrus (DG)-like sensory responses with CA3-like sequential memory. Temporal relationships between encountered sensory events provide two complementary learning signals: a **“push”** that reinforces DG activity according to the temporal separation between events, and a **“pull”** that motivates the policy to reach internally defined sensory targets. The agent learns from egocentric visual experience and intrinsic rewards, without explicit information about its physical location or spatial distances.

During exploration of a continuous visual environment, spatially informative DG activity emerges. To isolate the contribution of temporal information, we compare temporally weighted DG feedback against mean-matched constant feedback, evaluating the resulting representations on identical sensory observations. Temporal feedback produces higher DG spatial information, less redundant response maps, and a broader spatial distribution of field peaks across five paired training seeds. These improvements extend to downstream decoder representations, where spatial information also increases consistently. In contrast, temporally weighting the policy's target-hit reward does not consistently improve exploration or spatial representation quality compared with constant hit rewards.

The agent's behavior further influences the expression of its learned representations. Even with network weights frozen, the learned policy produces more spatially informative downstream activity than random actions, demonstrating that spatial coding depends not only on sensory representations but also on how an agent interacts with its environment.

Together, these results demonstrate that spatial representations can emerge from temporally structured sensory experience and non-spatial learning objectives in a hippocampus-inspired circuit. They illustrate how temporal learning and self-generated behavior can jointly contribute to spatial organization without explicitly optimizing for space.

## Comments to resolve before submission

1. **Learning-course claim.** The increase in DG spatial score is from policy-sampled online data. A multi-seed common-observation checkpoint series is still needed to say that the *fixed-input representation* improves throughout training. Current wording keeps that distinction.
2. **Behavioral causality.** The own-policy versus random-action intervention freezes weights. It supports a policy effect on *expression* of the learned code. A training-policy intervention would be needed to claim that the policy *refines the DG weights during learning*. The abstract stays with the expression result. Recurrent memory may mediate the policy effect, but the intervention did not isolate that mechanism.
3. **Pull ablation and control payoff.** The fresh C05 [temporal-versus-constant target-hit comparison](../06_experiments/cosyne_2027/04_worker_bonus_and_scope.md) tests the explicit temporal weighting of the “pull.” A constant hit bonus still rewards reaching a target, and actor-critic discounting still favors earlier hits, so this comparison weakens temporal shaping rather than removing pull. The five-seed coverage and fixed-observation DG-field effects are mixed or unfavorable to temporal bonus. The new [CA3 and decoder analysis on identical observations](../06_experiments/cosyne_2027/05_downstream_spatial_information.md) likewise finds mixed bits-per-activation contrasts and lower amplitude-weighted decoder scores in all five temporal-encoder worker pairs. The prospective graph effect is stronger in one encoder arm, but executed-command results are pending. Earlier bonus-versus-no-bonus controls also changed reward magnitude and crossed a source boundary. The newly submitted C15 constant-hit arm remains pending in the [run index](../06_experiments/cosyne_2027/README.md). Matched-start command outcomes and physical destinations remain open, so the abstract does not claim improved goal following.

   The fresh decoder-1 [behavior-expression figure](https://github.com/xiaoxionglin/IntrMotiv/blob/6412596/06_experiments/results/cosyne_2027_compact_figures/02_behavior_expression_fresh.png) and [seed table](https://github.com/xiaoxionglin/IntrMotiv/blob/6412596/06_experiments/results/cosyne_2027_behavior_secondary/all_training_seeds.csv) show a **lead**, not yet a clear representation benefit from temporal pull. At fixed temporal/constant DG credit, temporal worker credit raises the mean *own-policy minus random-policy* decoder-1 information contrast by +0.055/+0.187 bits per activation, but only 3/5 and 4/5 paired seeds favor it. In the underlying [evaluation table](https://github.com/xiaoxionglin/IntrMotiv/blob/6412596/06_experiments/results/cosyne_2027_behavior_secondary/fresh_pair_analysis/paired_evaluation_repeats.csv), the trained policy's absolute decoder-1 information changes by just +0.011/+0.042 bits per activation, with 3/5 positive seeds in each DG-credit arm. Every C05 factor cell favors its own policy over random actions, including constant pull. Keep this within-cell behavior effect distinct from the temporal-versus-constant worker contrast; the fresh figure comes from branch `codex/cosyne-behavior-fresh-seeds` at commit `6412596` and is not yet on `main`.
4. **Longer credit is a separate intervention.** The [episode-long comparison](../06_experiments/dg_goal_control/episode_long_dg_comparison_20261009.md#3-did-longer-credit-source-timing-or-goal-only-value-solve-sparse-hit-learning) jointly changes PPO $\gamma$ from 0.99 to 0.999 and GAE $\lambda$ from 0.95 to 0.99; it does not isolate discounting or test whether discounting substitutes for the explicit temporal hit bonus. At 100M, coverage increases in all three C15 seeds, while learned-unit spatial information on separate frozen policy rollouts falls by 0.051, 0.044, and 0.075 bits. Different occupancy can contribute to those measured field differences. This result is complementary to the fresh C05 bonus-timing factorial and must not be pooled with it as one ablation.
5. **Space and historical style.** I removed the crossed-out second closing sentence: it repeated the distinction already made by the results, and the updated abstract is approximately 321 words. The attached 2026 example uses a 296-word summary followed by figures. Confirm the current submission requirements and shorten the opening summary if needed.
