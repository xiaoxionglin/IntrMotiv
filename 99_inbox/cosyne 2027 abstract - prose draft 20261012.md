## Abstract

How can spatial representations emerge from egocentric experience without a map, position labels, or external reward? We propose that a hippocampal-inspired circuit can turn the timing of encountered sensory events into a learning signal. In our agent, sparse dentate gyrus (DG) responses mark salient visual experiences, while CA3-like sequences carry their recent history. Temporal separation between events creates two complementary pressures: a “push” that favors distinct DG landmarks and a “pull” that rewards the policy for reaching internally defined targets. Spatial structure can thus arise through the agent’s interaction with the world rather than being imposed as a training target.

In a continuous visual arena, the agent develops spatially informative DG activity during exploration. Replacing temporal DG feedback with constant feedback makes DG responses less spatially informative, more redundant, and concentrated in fewer parts of the arena. The benefit extends to downstream representations. This supports a role for temporal pattern separation in organizing the code. The policy-side “pull” is shaped by temporal target-hit credit and actor-critic discounting. Replacing the temporally weighted target-hit bonus with a constant bonus has mixed effects on exploration and DG fields. Actor-critic discounting can largely substitute for the bonus's timing effect by favoring faster traversal. Weakening that discount worsens measured spatial information.

The agent's interactions with the world also shape how its learned code is expressed. With weights frozen, the learned policy evokes more spatially informative downstream responses than random actions.

Together, these results show how temporal memory and self-directed action can produce spatial organization without direct spatial instruction.

## Comments to resolve before submission

1. **Learning-course claim.** The increase in DG spatial score is from policy-sampled online data. A multi-seed common-observation checkpoint series is still needed to say that the *fixed-input representation* improves throughout training. Current wording keeps that distinction.
2. **Behavioral causality.** The own-policy versus random-action intervention freezes weights. It supports a policy effect on *expression* of the learned code. A training-policy intervention would be needed to claim that the policy *refines the DG weights during learning*. The abstract stays with the expression result. Recurrent memory may mediate the policy effect, but the intervention did not isolate that mechanism.
3. **Pull ablation and control payoff.** The fresh C05 [temporal-versus-constant target-hit comparison](../06_experiments/cosyne_2027/04_worker_bonus_and_scope.md) tests the explicit temporal weighting of the “pull.” A constant hit bonus still rewards reaching a target, and actor-critic discounting still favors earlier hits, so this comparison weakens temporal shaping rather than removing pull. The five-seed coverage and fixed-observation DG-field effects are mixed or unfavorable to temporal bonus. The new [CA3 and decoder analysis on identical observations](../06_experiments/cosyne_2027/05_downstream_spatial_information.md) likewise finds mixed bits-per-activation contrasts and lower amplitude-weighted decoder scores in all five temporal-encoder worker pairs. The prospective graph effect is stronger in one encoder arm, but executed-command results are pending. Earlier bonus-versus-no-bonus controls also changed reward magnitude and crossed a source boundary. The newly submitted C15 constant-hit arm remains pending in the [run index](../06_experiments/cosyne_2027/README.md). Matched-start command outcomes and physical destinations remain open, so the abstract does not claim improved goal following.

   The fresh decoder-1 [behavior-expression figure](https://github.com/xiaoxionglin/IntrMotiv/blob/6412596/06_experiments/results/cosyne_2027_compact_figures/02_behavior_expression_fresh.png) and [seed table](https://github.com/xiaoxionglin/IntrMotiv/blob/6412596/06_experiments/results/cosyne_2027_behavior_secondary/all_training_seeds.csv) show a **lead**, not yet a clear representation benefit from temporal pull. Every C05 factor cell favors its own policy over random actions, including constant pull. Keep this within-cell behavior effect distinct from the temporal-versus-constant worker contrast; the fresh figure comes from branch `codex/cosyne-behavior-fresh-seeds` at commit `6412596` and is not yet on `main`.
4. **Longer credit is a separate intervention.** The [episode-long comparison](../06_experiments/dg_goal_control/episode_long_dg_comparison_20261009.md#3-did-longer-credit-source-timing-or-goal-only-value-solve-sparse-hit-learning) jointly changes PPO $\gamma$ from 0.99 to 0.999 and GAE $\lambda$ from 0.95 to 0.99; it does not isolate discounting or test whether discounting substitutes for the explicit temporal hit bonus. At 100M, coverage increases in all three C15 seeds, while learned-unit spatial information on separate frozen policy rollouts falls by 0.051, 0.044, and 0.075 bits. Different occupancy can contribute to those measured field differences. This result is complementary to the fresh C05 bonus-timing factorial and must not be pooled with it as one ablation.
5. **Space and historical style.** I removed the crossed-out second closing sentence: it repeated the distinction already made by the results, and the abstract is about 280 words without it. The attached 2026 example uses a 296-word summary followed by figures. Confirm whether the current submission instructions still allow a roughly 300-word opening summary.
